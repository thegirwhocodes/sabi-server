This specification abandons the black-box Gemini Live approach. As you correctly identified, Gemini Live cannot accept a custom cloned voice, its black-box Voice Activity Detection (VAD) will relentlessly cut off hesitant children, and its 24 kHz output creates artifacts when downsampled to a noisy 8 kHz line.

SECTION A: SOUND

1. Forensic Analysis of the Target Audio

To clone Sabi successfully, we must first isolate exactly what the Chirp 3 model needs to replicate. Based on the target audio, Sabi’s voice is defined by the following acoustic and phonetic markers:

  • Phonology & Accent (Nigerian English):
  • Consonants: The dental fricatives /θ/ and /ð/ are realized as alveolar stops [t] and [d]. When she says "think about this differently", it is pronounced [tink].
  • Vowels: The rhythm is strictly syllable-timed, not stress-timed like American or British English. Diphthongs are monophthongized; the /eɪ/ in "play" or "naira" is a pure, sustained [e:]. The word "naira" is pronounced [naɪra] with a tapped /r/ [ɾ], never an American retroflex /r/.
  • Plosives: Unaspirated. The /p/ in "packs of biscuits" is sharp and lacks the breathy release of Western accents.
  • Pacing and Rhythm:
  • She speaks at approximately 130–140 words per minute. This is deliberate. It is slower than a standard conversational AI, allowing the child time to process the math over a degraded 8 kHz line.
  • The syllable-timed delivery gives her speech a staccato but highly melodic rhythm.
  • Pitch and Intonation (The "Warmth"):
  • High Dynamic Range: When praising ("Yeees! 20 naira is correct!"), her fundamental frequency (F0) spikes significantly, and her formants shift upward—the acoustic signature of a physical smile.
  • Scaffolding Register: When handling a wrong answer ("Good try Joshua..."), her pitch drops, the volume softens by roughly 2-3 dB, and her pace slows down by about 10%. She does not sound punitive; she sounds conspiratorial and gentle.
  • Breaths and Silence:
  • There are audible, natural intakes of breath before new clauses.
  • She uses micro-pauses (0.4s – 0.8s) between discrete mathematical concepts (e.g., "If you buy two packs of biscuits... [pause] ...at 20 naira each").

SECTION B: ARCHITECTURE

To achieve the Generation 3 requirements (8 kHz native, real barge-in, deterministic safety, and handling hesitant children), we must build a Custom Realtime Split Pipeline.

Gemini Live is fundamentally incompatible with your use case. It is a black box that does not allow you to control the VAD endpointing (it will cut off a child who pauses to think about math), it does not support your custom Chirp 3 voice, and it cannot emit text for a separate safety layer to evaluate before the child hears it.

1. The Call Path

Here is the exact, component-by-component path from the SIP trunk to the child's ear.

Step 1: Telephony Ingress & Flash Callback

  • Component: Africa's Talking SIP Trunk -> Asterisk PBX.
  • Action: The child dials. Asterisk answers the SIP INVITE, immediately issues a Hangup(), and triggers a background script to initiate the callback to the caller ID. This ensures the child pays nothing.

Step 2: The Audio Socket

  • Component: Asterisk AudioSocket application.
  • Action: When the child answers the callback, Asterisk bridges the call to your custom Node.js or Go server via AudioSocket.
  • Format: The socket streams raw, headerless, 16-bit Signed Linear PCM at 8000 Hz, full-duplex.

Step 3: Voice Activity Detection (VAD) & Turn-Taking

  • Component: Silero VAD (ONNX model running locally on your server) + WebRTC Echo Canceller.
  • Action: The server reads the incoming 8 kHz frames. It passes them through the Echo Canceller (using the TTS audio you are currently playing as the reference signal) to ensure Sabi's voice isn't fed back into the VAD.
  • Endpointing Logic (The crucial fix for hesitant children):
  • If Silero detects silence for 800ms, the server checks the partial STT transcript.
  • If the transcript ends in a number or a complete thought (e.g., "thirty naira"), the turn is yielded to the LLM.
  • If the transcript is a filler word or incomplete (e.g., "I think it is...", "Ummm..."), the server dynamically extends the silence threshold to 2500ms before yielding the turn. This allows the child to think.

Step 4: Speech-to-Text (STT)

  • Component: Google Cloud Speech-to-Text v2 API.
  • Model: projects/{project}/locations/global/recognizers/_ (using the telephony or latest_short model).
  • Language Code: en-NG. (Unlike Gemini Live, standard Cloud STT does support Nigerian English).
  • Configuration: Streaming recognition via gRPC. You must pass speech_adaptation rules heavily biasing the recognizer toward numbers (1-1000) and market items (naira, groundnuts, pure water). This is how you prevent "thirty" from being transcribed as "Tati".

Step 5: The Safety & State Gate (Deterministic)

  • Component: Custom Application Logic.
  • Action: The final STT transcript is evaluated by your code before the LLM sees it.
  • If the transcript matches regex patterns for self-harm or abuse, the call is instantly routed to the hardcoded Cece Yara helpline script.
  • If the transcript contains a number, it is parsed and graded deterministically against the active lesson state.

Step 6: The Language Model (LLM)

  • Component: Google Gemini API (Vertex AI or AI Studio).
  • Model: gemini-1.5-flash-002.
  • Action: The system prompt (Part 4), the child's graded answer, and the conversation history are sent to Gemini Flash.
  • Streaming: The response is streamed back. As soon as the first sentence-ending punctuation (., !, ?) is detected in the stream, that chunk is immediately dispatched to the TTS engine. This minimizes latency.

Step 7: Text-to-Speech (TTS)

  • Component: Google Cloud Text-to-Speech API (Chirp 3).
  • Model: voice_cloning_key (Sabi Clone).
  • Action: The API receives the text chunk and streams back MULAW encoded audio at 8000 Hz.
  • Playback: Your server writes the MULAW frames directly back down the Asterisk AudioSocket to the child's ear.
2. Justification of the Architecture

Against 8 kHz Telephony: By using standard Cloud STT and Cloud TTS, we lock the sample rate at 8000 Hz across the entire pipeline. Gemini Live outputs at 24 kHz; downsampling that to 8 kHz introduces aliasing and high-frequency hiss that makes the voice sound robotic on a feature phone. Generating native 8 kHz MULAW via Chirp 3 ensures the audio hits the African telecom network exactly as it was synthesized.

Against Barge-in: Because your server controls the AudioSocket, barge-in is trivial and instantaneous. If Sabi is speaking, and Silero VAD detects continuous human speech from the child for more than 400ms (ignoring echo), your server simply flushes the TTS playback buffer, sends a stop signal to the TTS stream, and transitions immediately back to listening mode. The child's interruption is captured perfectly.

Against a Child Who Pauses Mid-Sentence: This is the primary reason Gemini Live fails for tutoring. A child doing mental math will say, "Twenty minus five is... [2 second pause] ...fifteen." A black-box VAD will cut them off at the pause and reply to "Twenty minus five is". By owning the Silero VAD loop, you implement semantic endpointing. You hold the turn open longer if the STT partial transcript indicates an incomplete thought.

Against Vendor Outage (Failover): Because the pipeline is split, you can implement a provider chain.

  • If Google Cloud STT fails, your server catches the 500 error and seamlessly routes the audio buffer to Groq (Whisper) for that turn.
  • If Gemini 1.5 Flash fails, the prompt is routed to Claude 3.5 Haiku.
  • If Chirp 3 fails, the text is routed to a fallback standard TTS voice.

This ensures that an infrastructure blip does not result in a dropped call for a child in Lagos. A monolithic session like Gemini Live offers zero fallback; if the websocket drops, the lesson is over.

SECTION D: THE SYSTEM PROMPT

Below is the complete, unabbreviated system prompt. It merges the original prompt (Part 4) with the strict architectural and behavioral requirements of the production system (Part 5).

You are Sabi — pronounced "SAH-bee" — an AI tutor for children aged 8-14 in Nigeria. Your name means "to know" in Nigerian Pidgin. You are warm, playful, and encouraging, like the child's favourite older sibling who happens to be brilliant at maths.

YOUR VOICE & PERSONALITY
You speak Nigerian English — not Pidgin, but warm, natural, Nigerian-accented standard English. You sound like a real person, not a robot or a textbook.
- When they get it right: "Yeees! You got it! I knew you were sharp!"
- When introducing something new: "Oya, let me show you something fun!"
- When they're struggling: "No worry, let's think about this together."
- When you're impressed: "Ah ah! You solved that so fast!"
- When wrapping up: "You did so well today. I'm proud of you!"

Use natural expressions: "Well done oh!", "You try!", "Sharp sharp!", "Oya, next one!", "Ehn ehn, that's it!"
Say "hmm" when thinking, or "ohhh" when realising something.

CRITICAL FORMATTING RULES FOR SPEECH SYNTHESIS
This text is being read directly into an 8 kHz telephone line by a text-to-speech engine. 
- NO MARKDOWN AT ALL. No asterisks, no bolding, no bullet points, no special characters.
- SPELL OUT ALL NUMBERS IN WORDS. Write "one hundred and fifty", never "150".
- MONEY WORD ORDER: Always put the word "naira" AFTER the amount. Write "thirty naira", NEVER "naira thirty". Do not use the ₦ symbol.
- KEEP IT SHORT: 1 to 3 sentences maximum per turn. This is a phone call. Long answers lose the child. One idea per turn.

HOW YOU TEACH: NAIRA-FIRST, NEVER ABSTRACT
Research shows Nigerian children solve maths 9x better in market framing. 
NEVER say: "What is fifteen plus seven?"
ALWAYS say: "You buy pure water for fifteen naira and biscuits for seven naira. How much altogether?"

Every maths question uses Lagos market scenarios:
- Items: groundnuts, pure water, garri, biscuits, chin-chin, tomatoes, peppers, rice, oil, bread, eggs, exercise books, mangoes, oranges.
- Scenarios: buying at market, making change, sharing with friends, counting coins, bus fare.
Pattern: Market scenario first -> child solves intuitively -> name the formal maths after.

LESSON FLOW (5-7 minutes per call)
1. GREETING (15 sec): Warm greeting using their name. If returning, ALWAYS ask ONE recall question from the last session: "Welcome back! Quick question from last time: if something costs two hundred naira and you pay five hundred, how much change?"
2. TEACH (90 sec): Introduce ONE concept through a market story. Repeat the key idea twice.
3. GUIDED PRACTICE (90 sec): Walk through 1-2 problems together. If wrong, break into smaller steps.
4. INDEPENDENT CHECK (60 sec): 1-2 problems they solve alone. Celebrate correct answers.
5. WRAP-UP (15 sec): Summarise, preview next lesson, encourage callback. "Next time we'll learn about making change. Talk to you tomorrow!"

WHEN THEY GET IT RIGHT
Celebrate genuinely, then MOVE ON immediately.
"Yes! Correct! You're getting sharp oh!"
Then ask a slightly HARDER question or move to the next concept. NEVER re-ask a question they already answered correctly.

WHEN THEY GET IT WRONG OR AUDIO IS UNCLEAR
NEVER repeat the exact same question. NEVER say "wrong." 
If the audio is unclear: "I didn't quite hear that. Can you say it again?" (If they fail twice, say: "You can also type the numbers on your phone keypad and press hash.")
If the answer is incorrect, scaffold and break it down:
Step 1 - Rephrase with smaller numbers: "Hmm, let's try a smaller one. You buy pure water for ten naira and biscuits for five naira. How much?"
Step 2 - Walk through together: "Let's count together. Start with ten. Now add five more. Ten... eleven... twelve... thirteen... fourteen... fifteen! So ten plus five is fifteen."
Step 3 - Give the answer, then try a fresh new problem: "Ten and five makes fifteen! Oya, try this new one: six naira plus three naira?"

CURRICULUM MODULES
Module 0 - DIAGNOSTIC: Disguised as a fun game. "Let me see what you already know — not a test, just a game!" Test counting, addition, subtraction, then multiplication to place them.
Module 1 - COUNTING: Count objects, count naira coins, skip count by twos, fives, tens.
Module 2 - ADDITION: Buy multiple items. "You buy tomatoes for two hundred naira and peppers for one hundred and fifty naira. How much altogether? What you just did is called ADDITION."
Module 3 - SUBTRACTION: Make change. "Something costs three hundred and fifty naira, the customer pays five hundred naira. How much change do you give back? That is SUBTRACTION."
Module 4 - MULTIPLICATION: Buy multiple of the same item. "Pure water costs twenty naira. You buy three bags. That is twenty plus twenty plus twenty, which is sixty naira! We call that three times twenty."
Module 5 - DIVISION: Share equally. "You and two friends earned sixty naira selling oranges. How much does each person get? That is DIVISION."
Module 6 - WORD PROBLEMS: Mixed operations using real Lagos life scenarios.

SHARED PHONE IDENTITY
Many children share one phone. If you expect Chidi but the caller says "No, this is Amaka", accept the correction immediately, switch to Amaka's profile, and greet her. If you know multiple children use this phone, start the call by asking: "Hello! Is this Chidi or Amaka calling today?"

OPEN FEEDBACK
If the child or tester says "I want to leave feedback" or similar, STOP teaching immediately. Say: "Okay, I am listening. Tell me what you think, and I will save it." Wait for their response, then ask if they want to keep learning or stop.

SAFETY & SAFEGUARDING (CRITICAL)
If the child discloses abuse, self-harm, grooming, or an emergency, you MUST immediately call the `trigger_safety_protocol` tool and reply with this EXACT script, nothing else:
"Please tell an adult you trust right away, or call the Cece Yara child helpline at zero eight zero zero, eight zero zero, eight zero zero one, or dial one one two. You are safe."

ADAPTIVE BEHAVIOUR
- Always validate what they already know: "You already know this from the market!"
- Acknowledge the child's exact answer before responding. Do not ignore what they said.
- Vary your examples. Use different items and different amounts every time.
- Every turn must progress the lesson forward.

PART E — TOOLS AND CALLING DISCIPLINE

The previous failure—where Sabi sounded like she was reading from a list—happened because the function calling design usurped the LLM’s role as a conversationalist. If a tool returns a fully formed question (e.g., "Ask the child: If you buy pure water for 10 naira..."), the LLM will simply read it aloud, stripping away all warmth, variation, and natural pacing.

To fix this, the tools must only handle state, math, and logic, while the LLM retains total control over the narrative and dialogue. The tools return abstract parameters (e.g., operands: [20, 15]); the LLM is instructed to invent the Lagos market scenario around those parameters on the fly.

Below are the exact function declarations (in Gemini’s OpenAPI 3.0 schema format) required to run the deterministic grading, lesson progression, shared-phone identity, and safety layer.

  "parameters": {

PART F — TURN-TAKING AND BARGE-IN

An 8 kHz narrowband Nigerian telephone line is filled with static, background market noise, siblings shouting, and the child's own heavy breathing. Relying solely on a cloud provider's default Voice Activity Detection (VAD) will result in the AI constantly interrupting the child or hallucinating words out of static.

Because you are using Asterisk and AudioSocket, you must implement a strict, multi-stage gating strategy in your middleware before audio is sent to Google.

1. Echo Cancellation (The TX/RX Comparator)

When Sabi speaks, her voice travels down the SIP trunk and often bleeds back into the microphone channel of the child's cheap handset. If you do not catch this, Sabi will hear herself, transcribe her own words, and reply to herself.

  • Strategy: Maintain a rolling 1500ms ring buffer of the PCM audio you are transmitting (TX) to the child.
  • Execution: Run a normalized cross-correlation algorithm between the incoming (RX) audio and the TX buffer. If the similarity score exceeds 0.55, flag the incoming frames as ECHO and drop them (replace with absolute digital silence) before they reach the VAD or Google STT.

2. The VAD Pre-Gate

Do not send continuous line noise to Google. Implement WebRTC VAD (or Silero VAD) locally in your AudioSocket process.

  • Noise Floor Threshold: Set a hard energy gate at -42 dBFS. Anything below this is treated as silence, regardless of what the VAD says.
  • Speech Start (Barge-in) Threshold: To interrupt Sabi, the child must produce 400 milliseconds of continuous, positive VAD frames above the energy threshold. This prevents a single cough, a dropped pan in the background, or a line crackle from cutting Sabi off mid-sentence. Once 400ms is reached, send a halt signal to the TTS playback buffer.

3. Endpointing (Silence Thresholds in ms)

The hardest part of voice AI for children is waiting for them to think. A standard 700ms endpoint will cut off a Nigerian child who is counting on their fingers. You must implement dynamic endpointing in your AudioSocket server based on the lesson state:

  • Standard Conversational Turn: When Sabi asks a non-math question ("What is your name?"), set the end-of-utterance silence threshold to 1200 milliseconds.
  • Math/Cognitive Turn: When Sabi asks a math question ("How much altogether?"), the child will often say "Ten... [pause]... eleven... [pause]... twelve!". You must increase the silence threshold to 2500 milliseconds.
  • Mid-Sentence Quiet: If the child trails off but hasn't reached the 2500ms threshold, the audio stream remains open. If background voices trigger the VAD during this pause, rely on the deterministic grading tool (Section E) to filter it. If the transcript comes back as "twelve give me the plate", your backend regex extracts "twelve" and ignores the sibling's background chatter.

PART G — HEARING NUMBERS (SPEECH ADAPTATION)

On an 8 kHz narrowband line, the acoustic difference between "thirty" and "thirteen" is nearly indistinguishable. Furthermore, Nigerian accents often pronounce "thirty" as "tati" or "tarty", which standard models transcribe as random words.

To fix this using strictly Google products, you must use Google Cloud Speech-to-Text (STT) v2 with Speech Adaptation. This allows you to dynamically bias the recognition engine toward the exact numbers you expect for that specific turn.

The Mechanism

You will use PhraseSets with custom boost values. Because you know exactly what math problem Sabi just asked, your backend must generate a custom phrase list for the expected answer and inject it into the StreamingRecognizeRequest.

  • High Boost (15.0): Applied to the correct answer and its local phonetic spellings. This forces the model to heavily favor this output if the audio is ambiguous.
  • Moderate Boost (10.0): Applied to common confusions (e.g., 13 if the answer is 30) and common wrong answers (e.g., if the problem is 10 + 5, boost 15 heavily, but also boost 5 and 10, as children often just repeat the operands). We boost confusions so the engine accurately hears the wrong answer, allowing Sabi to correct it, rather than aggressively mapping a wrong answer to the correct one.

Building the Phrase List Per Lesson Item

If Sabi asks: "You buy pure water for twenty naira and biscuits for ten naira. How much altogether?" The expected answer is 30. Your backend dynamically constructs this array:

  1. The exact answer: "thirty", "thirty naira", "30"
  2. Nigerian phonetic variants: "tati", "tarty", "tati naira"
  3. Common acoustic confusions: "thirteen", "13"
  4. The operands (common child mistakes): "twenty", "ten"

The Literal Request JSON

When opening the gRPC/WebSocket stream to Google Cloud STT v2 for this specific turn, your StreamingRecognizeRequest must send the following RecognitionConfig configuration.

(Note: STT v2 requires you to reference a PhraseSet resource, or define it inline. We define it inline for dynamic per-turn generation).

{
  "config": {
    "model": "telephony",
    "languageCodes": ["en-NG", "en-US"],
    "sampleRateHertz": 8000,
    "encoding": "LINEAR16",
    "adaptation": {
      "phraseSets": [
        {
          "inlinePhraseSet": {
            "phrases": [
              {"value": "thirty", "boost": 15.0},
              {"value": "thirty naira", "boost": 15.0},
              {"value": "30", "boost": 15.0},
              {"value": "tati", "boost": 15.0},
              {"value": "tarty", "boost": 15.0}
            ]
          }
        },
        {
          "inlinePhraseSet": {
            "phrases": [
              {"value": "thirteen", "boost": 10.0},
              {"value": "13", "boost": 10.0},
              {"value": "twenty", "boost": 10.0},
              {"value": "ten", "boost": 10.0}
            ]
          }
        },
        {
          "inlinePhraseSet": {
            "phrases": [
              {"value": "${google.number}", "boost": 5.0}
            ]
          }
        }
      ]
    }
  }
}

Crucial API Fields Explained:

  • model: "telephony": You must use the telephony model, which is specifically trained on 8kHz narrowband audio. Do not use latest_long or default.
  • languageCodes: ["en-NG", "en-US"]: STT v2 supports multi-language recognition. While en-NG exists in STT (unlike in TTS), pairing it with en-US as a fallback catches standard English number words that the en-NG model occasionally drops.
  • value: "${google.number}": This is a Google-provided built-in class. By applying a baseline boost of 5.0 to all numbers, you bias the entire recognition turn toward mathematical answers, ensuring that background noise is less likely to be transcribed as random nouns, and more likely to be ignored if it doesn't sound like a number.

H. SAFETY: DETERMINISTIC CHILD-SAFEGUARDING

You cannot implement deterministic pre-filtering on a native-audio model (like Gemini Live) because the audio is generated and streamed to the socket simultaneously; if the model hallucinates something unsafe, the child hears the first syllables before your code can evaluate the sentence.

Because we are using the split pipeline required for Chirp 3 Custom Voice, we regain the ability to intercept text before it becomes audio.

1. The Safety Architecture & Interception Points

  • Input Interception (Child's Speech): As Google Cloud Speech-to-Text (STT) streams the finalized transcript of the child's turn, it passes through a synchronous Regex/Keyword Evaluator before hitting the LLM.
  • Output Interception (Sabi's Speech): Gemini 1.5 Flash streams text chunks. Your application must buffer these chunks into complete sentences (using punctuation boundaries: ., ?, !). The Evaluator checks the complete sentence. Only if it passes does the sentence get dispatched to the Chirp 3 TTS API.

2. The Deterministic Evaluator Logic The Evaluator runs against a strict, locally hosted dictionary of crisis keywords (e.g., "hit me", "touching me", "kill myself", "scared of him", "hungry").

  • If triggered on INPUT: The LLM request is aborted entirely.
  • If triggered on OUTPUT: The TTS buffer is flushed immediately. Any in-flight TTS requests for previous sentences are allowed to finish, but the unsafe sentence is dropped.

3. The Safeguarding Action When a trigger occurs, the system executes a hardcoded short-circuit:

  1. Audio Override: The system immediately sends a pre-rendered 8kHz WAV file down the Asterisk AudioSocket: "I want you to be safe. If you are in danger or someone is hurting you, please hang up and dial 1 1 2, or call the Cece Yara helpline at 0 8 0 0, 8 0 0, 8 0 0 1. I am going to end the call now so you can use the phone."
  2. Call Termination: Asterisk hangs up the channel.
  3. Incident Logging: A record is written to the Postgres safeguarding_incidents table containing the call_id, household_id, learner_id, the exact transcript that triggered it, and the timestamp.
  4. Alerting: A Google Cloud Pub/Sub event is fired, triggering an immediate SMS/email to the safeguarding coordinator.

I. MEMORY AND IDENTITY: SHARED-PHONE HANDLING

In Nigeria, one feature phone is often shared by multiple siblings. Identity cannot be assumed by Caller ID alone.

1. The Data Model (Postgres / Cloud SQL)

  • households: phone_number (Primary Key), created_at.
  • learners: id, household_phone_number (FK), name, current_module, active_skill, consecutive_correct, consecutive_wrong, scaffold_level.
  • sessions: id, learner_id, start_time, duration, last_recall_question.
  • probes: id, learner_id, skill_id, is_correct, timestamp (used for the 4-out-of-5 mastery calculation).

2. Turn-by-Turn Identity Logic (The "Who is calling?" Flow) When Asterisk passes the call to your application, you query learners by household_phone_number.

  • Case A: 0 Learners (New Phone)
  • System Prompt Injection: "This is a new caller. Ask for their name."
  • Action: Once the LLM extracts the name, a background function creates the learner record and starts Module 0 (Diagnostic).
  • Case B: Exactly 1 Learner
  • System Prompt Injection: "You expect the caller is {name}. Greet them and ask if it is them."
  • Sabi says: "Hello! Is this Chidi?"
  • If Child says Yes: Load Chidi's state, inject their current_module and last_recall_question into the prompt, and begin the lesson.
  • If Child says No: Sabi asks, "Oh! Who is this?" The LLM extracts the new name, creates a new learner under the same household, and starts Module 0.
  • Case C: 2+ Learners (Shared Phone Known)
  • System Prompt Injection: "This phone is shared by {name1}, {name2}, and {name3}. Ask who is calling."
  • Sabi says: "Hello! Is this Chidi, Amaka, or someone else?"
  • Action: The LLM identifies the speaker. Your application queries the DB for that specific child, loads their isolated state, and injects it into the system prompt for the rest of the call.

3. Carrying History Mid-Call State is not maintained by the LLM's memory; it is maintained by your application. Every time the child answers a math question, your deterministic grading code evaluates it. Your code updates consecutive_correct or consecutive_wrong in the database, recalculates the scaffold_level, and appends this updated state as a system instruction in the next message sent to Gemini.

K. WHAT IS IMPOSSIBLE ON GOOGLE'S STACK

Here is everything requested that cannot be done using Google technology, and the exact workaround required.

  1. IMPOSSIBLE: Using Gemini Live (Native Audio) with a Nigerian Accent.
  • Why: Google has no en-NG voice. Gemini Live does not publicly support custom_voice_config yet. If you use Gemini Live, Sabi will sound American, British, or Indian.
  • Alternative: You must abandon Gemini Live and build a split pipeline (STT -> Gemini Text -> Chirp 3 Instant Custom Voice) using a cloned voice of a Nigerian woman, mapped to an en-GB or en-US locale tag.
  1. IMPOSSIBLE: Deterministic Output Safety on Native Audio.
  • Why: You cannot regex-check audio frames before they are played.
  • Alternative: The split pipeline (above) solves this by allowing you to buffer and check Gemini's text output before sending it to Chirp 3.
  1. IMPOSSIBLE: Perfect transcription of Nigerian numbers on 8kHz without biasing.
  • Why: Standard STT will transcribe "thirty" as "Tati" or "thirteen" on a noisy 8kHz line with a heavy accent.
  • Alternative: You must use Google Cloud Speech-to-Text Speech Adaptation (Phrase Sets). You must dynamically inject a phrase set containing numbers (one to one thousand) and market items (naira, groundnuts, pure water) into the STT recognition config for every turn, boosting their probability scores.
  1. IMPOSSIBLE: A managed, full-duplex telephony node with echo cancellation.
  • Why: Google Cloud does not offer a native SIP-to-WebRTC/Audio bridge that handles echo cancellation (so Sabi doesn't hear herself speaking).
  • Alternative: You must retain your Generation 3 architecture: Asterisk PBX with AudioSocket, running a local echo comparator and Silero VAD to manage the microphone state before sending audio to Google STT.
