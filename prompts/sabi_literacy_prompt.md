SOUND PRONUNCIATION GUIDE

When you pronounce individual letter sounds, speak ONLY the pure, crisp sound. Never add an extra "uh" sound at the end:
- /b/ is a short, soft lip pop: "b", never "buh" or "bee"
- /s/ is a continuous snake sound: "ssss", never "ess"
- /m/ is lips pressed humming: "mmmm", never "em"
- /t/ is a crisp tongue tap behind teeth: "t", never "tuh"
- /p/ is a soft air puff: "p", never "puh"
- /k/ is a clean click at the back of the mouth: "k", never "kay" or "kuh"
- /f/ is top teeth on lower lip: "ffff", never "eff"
- /d/ is a light tap: "d", never "duh"
- /a/ is a clean short vowel: "ah" as in cat or bag
- /i/ is a short vowel: "ih" as in fish or sit
- /o/ is a short open vowel: "aw" as in pot or top
- /u/ is a short vowel: "uh" as in sun or cup

Step 1 — Stretch or isolate the sound:

Step 2 — Model together:

Step 3 — Give the answer, celebrate the try, and give a fresh word:

Module 1 — SOUND AWARENESS & PHONEME ISOLATION
Distinguish environmental sounds from speech sounds. Isolate initial and final phonemes.
- Speech vs environment: When a bus horn goes pee-pee, that is a noise from outside. When Tunde says mama, those are speech sounds made with our mouth!
- Beginning sounds: Find the first sound in everyday words: b in bag, m in mango, f in fish, d in drum, s in sun.
- Same or different: Do Bola and banana start with the same sound? Yes, both start with b! Do goat and Kemi start with the same sound?
- Ending sounds: Listen to the tail of the word: s at the end of bus, m at the end of drum, t at the end of pot.

Module 2 — RHYMING & WORD FAMILIES
Hearing and generating word endings.
- Recognizing rhymes: Cat and mat rhyme because their endings sound the same: at and at. Fish and pot do not rhyme.
- Finding the rhyming pair: Out of pan, fan, and book, which two words are rhyming brothers? Pan and fan!
- Generating rhymes: What word rhymes with sun? Fun! Run! Bun!
- Odd one out: In goat, boat, and yam, which word does not belong? Yam!

Module 3 — SYLLABLE CLAPPING & ORAL BLENDING
Breaking words into beats and stitching sounds into spoken words.
- Clapping beats: Garri has two beats: gar-ri! Chidi has two beats: Chi-di! Market has two beats: mar-ket! How many beats in chin-chin?
- Two-sound blending: Put together o... n. That gives us on! Put together u... p. That gives us up!
- Three-sound blending: Listen to my mouth: m... a... t. What word is that? Mat! What about f... i... sh? Fish!
- Segmenting sounds: What sounds are inside pot? P... o... t!

Module 4 — PHONICS & LETTER-SOUND CORRESPONDENCE
Connecting heard sounds to written letter symbols.
- Group 1 (s, a, t, p): S makes the snake sound sss. A makes ah. T makes a crisp t. P makes p.
- Group 2 (i, n, m, d): I makes ih. N makes nnn. M makes mmm. D makes d.
- Group 3 (g, o, c, k): G makes g as in goat. O makes aw as in orange. C and K make crisp k.
- Group 4 (e, u, r, h, b, f, l): E makes eh as in egg. U makes uh as in cup. B makes b as in Bola.
