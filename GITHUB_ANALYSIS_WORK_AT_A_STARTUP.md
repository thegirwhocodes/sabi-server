## What YC Startups Actually Look For (Research Findings)

### Top Signals (Ranked by Importance)

1. **Shipping velocity** — Can you take vague requirements and get something working this week?
2. **Ownership depth** — Do you drive problems through to completion without hand-holding?
3. **Product thinking** — Have you actually tried the product? Can you propose specific improvements?
4. **Debugging from symptoms** — Can you work backwards from user-visible issues through layers?
5. **Communication density** — Do you write clear, decision-dense updates?
6. **Comfort with ambiguity** — Can you make irreversible decisions on bad information?
7. **Full-stack pragmatism** — Can you touch any layer without fear?

### What Matters Less Than You Think

- LeetCode/algorithm mastery (they test practical debugging instead)
- Framework expertise (systems thinking > syntax)
- Perfect abstractions (working code > elegant architecture)
- Pedigree (slope > credentials)
- Contribution graph green squares (quality > quantity)

**Source:** [How to Interview at a YC Startup](https://standout.work/blog/how-to-interview-at-a-yc-startup), [How to Evaluate a Founding Engineer in 2026](https://tanayshah.dev/blog/how-to-evaluate-a-founding-engineer-2026/)

## Interview Preparation (Beyond GitHub)

Research shows YC interviews focus on:

### 1. **Have You Used the Product?**

**The #1 killer question:** "What interests you in our company?"

**80% of candidates fail by saying:** "I love your mission" or "the team seems great"

**What wins:** "I tried your product yesterday. I noticed [specific observation 1], [observation 2], and [observation 3]. If I joined, the first thing I'd ship is [specific feature] because [reason]."

**Your action:** Before EVERY interview, spend 30 minutes using the product and write down:
- 3 specific observations
- 1 feature you'd build first
- Why it matters to their users

---

### 2. **Practical Debugging, Not LeetCode**

**What they ask:**
- "Here's a production bug where users see X. Walk me through how you'd debug it."
- "Here's our codebase. Fix this bug in the next hour."
- "Build a mini-feature over the weekend and present it."

**What they're screening for:**
- Do you work backwards from symptoms?
- Can you read unfamiliar code?
- Do you ask clarifying questions?
- Can you ship something usable quickly?

**Your action:** Practice "production debugging from symptoms" — not algorithms.

---

### 3. **Communication Density**

**The test:** "Summarize a 300-line PR you shipped last week in three sentences."

**What they want:** One paragraph with the what, why, and tradeoff.

**Example:**
"Added webhook retry logic with exponential backoff. Customers were losing events on transient network failures. Chose SQLite job queue over Redis for simplicity since we're <10k req/s — can swap later if needed."

**Your action:** Practice explaining your projects in 3-sentence summaries.
